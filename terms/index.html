Using the site

The site is free to use for learning, teaching and personal interest. Please use it fairly: it reads data from NASA's public services, so automated or heavy use should go to NASA's APIs directly, with your own key.

Not for safety decisions

The data comes from NASA, NASA/JPL and ESA, but it can be estimated, delayed, simplified or wrong, and the site can make mistakes in showing it. Asteroid sizes, for example, are often estimated from brightness. Nothing on the site is an official warning or forecast. For official information about asteroid close approaches and impact risk, see NASA/JPL's Center for Near Earth Object Studies.

Sky Finder and looking at the sky

Sky Finder and the "Visible from here" tab help you find things in the night sky. Using them, you agree that:

  • You never point your eyes, binoculars, a telescope or a camera at the Sun. Looking at the Sun through any optical aid causes instant and permanent eye damage. Sky Finder marks the Sun in red so it can be avoided; it does not make looking at it safe.
  • You look after your own safety outdoors at night: watch where you walk, stay away from roads, water, edges and private land, and never use Sky Finder while driving or cycling.
  • Directions, times and brightness are estimates for learning and enjoyment. A phone's compass and motion sensors can be several degrees off, positions are worked out from published data that can be out of date, and an object may not be visible at all from your place, weather and sky.

The spacecraft map

Positions on the spacecraft map are worked out from NASA/JPL Horizons and from CelesTrak's published orbital elements; satellite positions grow less accurate as the elements age, and are not for navigation or any operational use. Mission descriptions are summaries; each agency's own pages, linked from each craft, are the authority. Names of missions and agencies belong to their owners; the map uses no agency's logo or insignia.
