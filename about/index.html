About this site

About

NASA and ESA Data Projects is a free website for learning about space and Earth with real data, straight from NASA and ESA (the European Space Agency). It turns their open data and APIs into things you can explore: tables, maps, charts, a globe and pictures.

The projects

  • Asteroid Tracker (live): the near-Earth objects passing Earth this week and every year since 2000, a page and orbit diagram for each, a search, and a map of all of them with the planets and spacecraft, with impact-risk lists from both NASA/JPL and ESA side by side. Distances are also given in Moon distances, so they are easy to picture.
  • Image Explorer (live): the whole Earth from orbit, day by day, from NASA's satellite imagery; a globe with natural events, astronaut photos and the sky; and the picture libraries of NASA and of ESA/Webb and ESA/Hubble, searchable by subject, mission and date.
  • Mars Data (live): the weather measured on the surface of Mars by NASA's Curiosity rover since 2012 and Perseverance since 2021, cleaned by written rules and organised by Mars day, season and hour, with charts that show the patterns of a Martian year and a page on how the data is made.
  • Sky Finder and the spacecraft map: a phone app that points to tonight's passing asteroid, and the active spacecraft of NASA, ESA and other agencies, from low Earth orbit to the Voyagers.

How it works

  • Real data. The numbers and pictures come from NASA and NASA/JPL services, with ESA's data added where it helps, either live as the page loads or from data files refreshed automatically every day. Every page says where its data comes from.
  • Runs in your browser. There is no account and nothing to install. The site is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, hosted on GitHub Pages.
  • Open to learn from. All of the code is public on GitHub under the MIT License, so anyone can read how it works and build on it.
