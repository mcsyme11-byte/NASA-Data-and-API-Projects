About

Why this exists

NASA's rovers on Mars carry small weather stations that measure the air every second: its pressure, its temperature, the warmth of the ground, the humidity and the sunlight. Over the years that adds up to billions of readings, published by NASA as tables for specialists.

This project takes those tables and turns them into something anyone can read. Every reading is checked against written rules, organised by Mars day (sol), season and hour, summarised and drawn as charts, so that the patterns of the Martian year become visible.

It is a learning project: to practise the real work of data science, from raw files to clear pictures, on one of the longest weather records from another planet.

Real measurements

Every number comes from NASA's archive; nothing is simulated or smoothed into a model.

Open about every step

The cleaning rules are written down, and every batch of data has a report of what was kept and removed.

Kept up to date

New releases from NASA are checked with the same rules and added.

Used on every chart

Four words first

Sol
A Mars day: 24 h 39 min. Each rover counts sols from its landing, so Curiosity's sol 1 was August 7, 2012.
Ls (season)
Where Mars is on its orbit, 0–360°. Ls 0° starts northern spring; Ls 90° northern summer; Ls 180° autumn; Ls 270° winter (summer in the south).
Mars Year
One trip around the Sun: 687 Earth days. Numbered from 1955; Curiosity landed in Mars Year 31.
Pascal (Pa)
The unit of air pressure. Earth's air at sea level: about 101,000 Pa. Gale Crater: about 700–950 Pa, under 1 %.
